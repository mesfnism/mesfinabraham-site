Do Ethiopian job ads say what the job pays?

Most Ethiopian job ads never say what the job pays. This page counts how many do, which kinds of jobs are most open about it, and how the advertised pay changes with the education a job asks for.

Good to know

  • What counts as a salary figure. An ad counts when it gives a number, such as “16,780 Birr monthly” or “17,001 to 20,000”. Ads that only say “as per company scale” or “negotiable” are counted on their own and shown in the lighter colour.
  • Birr and months. When an ad gives a number with no currency or period, we assume Birr per month. The education chart uses only monthly Birr figures between 1,000 and 300,000 and leaves out other currencies.
  • Public sector jobs. These are government job announcements. Their pay follows a fixed salary scale by grade, so almost all of them give a figure. We show them on their own because the government sets that pay by rule and nobody negotiates it.
  • Comparing like with like. To compare ads that ask for the same experience, we fit a straight line through the ads, working with percentage differences in pay instead of Birr amounts. Each extra year of study or experience then shows up as a percentage rise. The likely range is the span in which the true figure falls 95 times out of 100.
  • Small numbers. We hide a percentage when a row has fewer than 30 ads, and a dot on the education chart when fewer than 10 ads give a figure.
  • A partial picture. Employers who give a salary are not typical of all employers. They hire more often for entry level, contract and certificate level jobs. The gaps between groups tell you how jobs get advertised, and only roughly what people earn.
  • Where the figures come from. We collect ads every day from Ethiopian job websites and government announcements. An ad that appears on several sites is counted once, and it is dated by the day we first found it.
